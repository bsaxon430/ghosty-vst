Ghosty Spectrum: User Manual

Ghosty Spectrum is a sound maker you play with the mouse. You paint ringing notes, draw loops, paint the sound itself on a spectrogram, turn lines into gliding voices, reshape a voice, and then put it all on a timeline or drag it straight into your DAW.

  • Version: 3.1.0
  • Formats: VST3 (Windows and Mac), AU (Mac), Standalone app. The Mac installer also carries a CLAP build when one was built. The Windows installer has no CLAP.
  • Type: instrument (stereo out). It listens to MIDI notes (all channels). It sends no MIDI out. It has one optional stereo sidechain input, used only by DETECT KEY (in the Standalone app that input is off by default so the microphone is not opened).
  • Window: 1200 x 790 pixels plus the top bar. It can be resized from 75% to 200% with the shape kept, and everything scales together.
  • Looks: BLACK, PURPLE, LIGHT.

1. Quick start

Make your first sound in 2 minutes

  1. Load Ghosty Spectrum as an instrument in your DAW (see FL STUDIO TIPS).
  2. Press the ? at the far left of the tab bar. This opens HOW IT WORKS: a card called WHAT IS SPECTRUM and one card per tab, each with steps, gestures, what can be dragged out, and a TRY IT button that loads a small built-in example (made by the plugin, no recordings) and takes you there. Use the left and right arrow keys, or NEXT and PREVIOUS, to move between cards. Esc closes it.
  3. Click the PAINT PADS tab. Click a STARTING POINT on the left (for example Bell chord). It plays at once and keeps ringing.
  4. Click anywhere on the canvas to add more notes. Drag a note while it rings: up and down changes the pitch, sideways changes the tone.
  5. Turn BLOOM, SPACE and SHIMMER at the bottom to make it bigger.
  6. Click SEND TO ARRANGE (bottom right). The sound lands on the timeline as a clip.
  7. Click the ARRANGE tab, press PLAY (or SPACE), and move the clip around.
  8. When you like it, drag DRAG PATTERN OUT (WAV) (PAINT pages) or the MIX chip (ARRANGE) into your DAW's playlist. Dragging files out is part of the full version; the demo shows an UNLOCK hint instead (see DEMO VS LICENSED).
Nothing playing? Look at the top bar. SYNC: OWN, PLAY: ON NOTES and BYPASS all change when Spectrum makes sound. See TROUBLESHOOTING.

First-run hints

The first time you open a tab, a hint bubble tells you the first thing to do ("First: ..."). Its buttons are GOT IT (hide it for good) and a second button that opens that page's HOW IT WORKS card. Your first click on the page also hides it. The plugin remembers this in the project.

2.2 PAINT PADS

PAINT PADS is a canvas of ringing notes. Every dot you paint is a note that keeps sounding.

  • Up and down is pitch. The canvas runs from F#1 at the bottom to A7 at the top. The root note of your key is marked on the right edge in green, and every C is labelled in grey. Middle C is C4.
  • Left and right is tone. A strip along the bottom names five zones, from left to right: PURE, SOFT, WARM, BRIGHT, DRIVEN. Dots further right get more overtones and more drive.
  • Dots talk to each other. Two dots whose overtones almost line up "beat" against each other. Drag one until they agree and the beating settles into harmony.

PAINT PADS and PAINT LOOP share the same top rows, the same patterns A-H and the same SAVE file. Each page keeps its own drawing in each pattern: dots on PAINT PADS, notes on PAINT LOOP. Both play together.

How the pad plays (DAW, PLAY and the loop)

  • With HOLD on, the pad sounds as soon as you paint. You don't need to press play.
  • The pattern length (1, 2 or 4 bars) is the loop length. While your DAW plays, or PLAY is on, BELL and PIANO dots ring again at the start of every loop (strummed by STRUM). They also ring when playback starts. With nothing playing they ring once and fade. PAD and FEEDBACK simply keep holding.
  • The loop is counted from the start of your song, at the DAW's tempo (or the own tempo in SYNC: OWN).
  • PLAY: ON NOTES (top bar): a pad with HOLD on follows the note gate. It only sounds while a MIDI note is held. With HOLD off, the pad already follows your keys.

MIDI keys and the pad

  • HOLD off: the pad sounds while at least one MIDI key is held and fades when you let go.
  • Transpose: the lowest held key transposes the whole pad. The key's root note at middle C (C4 when the key is C, D4 when it is D, and so on) plays the pad as painted. One key higher is a semitone up. This works with HOLD on too. The pad stays at the last transposition after you let go.
  • Re-ring: every key you press rings the BELL and PIANO dots again, louder with harder velocity, strummed low to high.
  • Pressing a key also restarts a pad stopped with STOP.
  • When SPECTRAL LAB is open with KEYS on, your keys play the Lab instead of the pad.
  • MIDI keys do not play the PAINT LOOP sounds.

2.3 PAINT LOOP

PAINT LOOP is a piano roll you draw on. Up is pitch, across is time. The canvas spans roughly 20 Hz at the bottom to 20 kHz at the top. Lines mark 30, 60, 100, 200, 500, 1k, 2k, 5k, 10k and 16k Hz, and the left edge reads BASS, MIDS and AIR. Bright vertical lines mark bars, faint ones mark beats, and with QUANTIZE on even fainter lines show the grid. While playing, a playhead runs across, and the live sound glows as colour behind your notes.

The top rows (PLAY, tempo, length, SAVE, LOAD, CLEAR, patterns A-H, COPY, key, scale, SNAP TO KEY, DETECT KEY, UNLOCK) work as described in 2.2. CLEAR removes this pattern's notes but keeps the sounds. QUANTIZE and its grid box only show here.

Note details

  • A clicked or dropped note is 1 grid step long on a drum. On a synth or sample it is 2 grid steps long, but never shorter than half a beat.
  • All notes play at the same velocity (85%). There is no velocity editing.
  • Synths and samples: with SNAP TO KEY on, pitch lands on a note of the key. With it off, pitch lands on the nearest semitone.
  • Drums: a drum always lands on whole semitones and ignores SNAP TO KEY. Each drum has a home height (where its own energy sits in the spectrum). There it plays at its normal pitch. Higher plays it faster and higher, lower plays it slower and lower, up to two octaves either way. Drums play to their end (they are one-shots) and are drawn as round dots. Synth notes are drawn as bars.
  • Each sound has its own colour on the canvas and on its layer button.

2.4 SPECTRAL LAB

The SPECTRAL LAB is a spectrogram you can paint on. Up is pitch, across is time, and brighter is louder. You can import a sound or a picture, and it becomes the canvas. Then you can paint on it, edit it, play it from your MIDI keys or in time with the song, and send it out.

Open it with the SPECTRAL LAB tab. The tab's tooltip reads "Paint the sound itself: pictures to sound, ghosts, band rhythms, chaos, morph". While the Lab is open, it covers the whole window. Opening it stops a running PAINT PADS pad, so no drone plays under the Lab.

The screen has four parts:

  • the top strip: PLAY, HEARING, KEYS / LOOP, ONE-SHOT and the OUT meter;
  • the page buttons and view buttons above the canvas;
  • the left panel: TOOLS, BRUSH, KEY + PITCH, the canvas buttons and OUT;

MIDI and the Lab

  • MIDI reaches the Lab only while the Lab is open and KEYS is selected. Notes on any channel play it.
  • Root note: the key set in the root box (default C4) plays the sound at its own pitch. Other keys play it higher or lower, the SPECTRAL or TAPE way (see KEYS PITCH).
  • Voices: up to 12. When all are busy, the oldest one is replaced. Attack is about 2 ms and release about 60 ms. With ONE-SHOT on, note-offs are ignored and the whole sound plays.
  • Velocity sets the level: gain = 0.35 + 0.65 x velocity, so the softest note is about -9 dB. With PER NOTE on (DNA), velocity also sets the morph (soft = A, hard = B).
  • Ghost hit: while hearing GHOST, every new key press (and every loop pass in LOOP mode or PLAY) plays the next ghost, unless the hit box or Lab Ghost Hit picks one ghost.

2.5 PROCESS

PROCESS makes destructive changes to the SPECTRAL LAB's sound. You select a part of the waveform, build a chain of processes, listen with PREVIEW, and then keep the result. The page line reads: "Destructive processes on the SPECTRAL LAB's sound (48 kHz mono). Select a region, build a chain, PREVIEW, then APPLY - or BOUNCE NEW to keep the original."

What it works on. The Lab's current sound, at 48 kHz mono. If you imported a sound and have not painted on it, that sound is used as it is. Otherwise the Lab canvas is rendered at high quality. Make or import a sound in SPECTRAL LAB first. TRY IT on the PROCESS card does this for you: it makes a new Lab canvas (a minor chord that swells, then a rising glide; you can undo it in the Lab) and opens PROCESS.

When the Lab's sound changes somewhere else (you paint, import, undo in the Lab, pick a SOURCE, or use BOUNCE NEW), PROCESS loads the new sound again. The whole sound is then selected, and PROCESS's own undo history is cleared.

Basic flow

  1. Select. When a sound loads, all of it is selected. Drag across the waveform to select a part. A single click only sets a cursor (no region), and PREVIEW / APPLY / BOUNCE stay off until there is a region. Double-click the waveform, press A (or Ctrl/Cmd+A), or click ALL to select the whole sound again.
  2. + ADD a process from the menu and set its controls. The new process goes in right after the selected one, or at the end of the chain.
  3. PREVIEW (P) renders the chain on the region. AUTO (on by default) does this for you 150 ms after every change to the chain, the seed or the region. PLAY SRC plays the original, PLAY FX plays the result.
  4. APPLY (Enter) keeps the result. The Lab now plays it. BOUNCE (Ctrl/Cmd+B) keeps it and clears the chain, so you can start again. BOUNCE NEW makes the result a new Lab sound and keeps the old one in the SOURCE menu.

  • Spectrum's own bar (top right of the page): the SOURCE menu and BACK TO THE LAB.
  • Waveform. It has a seconds ruler. When a tempo is known it also shows bars ("bar 1", "bar 2" ...). The selection is highlighted, and an info line shows REGION <start> s +<length> s of <total> s 48000 Hz 1 ch (or CURSOR <pos> s). Once a preview is ready, a lane under the waveform shows the result in place on the same time scale ("PREVIEW <length> s (in context)"): the original before it, the result, then the original after it.
  • Number row: a progress bar, START and LEN (the region in seconds; double-click to type a value), and the zoom buttons -, + and FIT.
  • Left: the chain list, with + ADD, DEL, DUP, UP, DOWN above it. Each row shows its number, the process name and its group.
  • Right: the parameters of the selected process: its name, its one-line description, and its controls.
  • Button row 1: PREVIEW, AUTO, PLAY SRC, PLAY FX, STOP, LOOP, DRAG, CANCEL.
  • Button row 2: UNDO, REDO, AGAIN, PER SLICE, BOUNCE NEW, BOUNCE, APPLY.

Seed

Processes that use chance follow the chain's SEED. The same chain, seed and sound always give the same result. Each process in the chain gets its own seed, made from the chain seed and its place in the chain. For a seeded process, the parameter panel shows "seeded: change SEED for another take".

Undo

UNDO and REDO in PROCESS step through APPLY, BOUNCE, AGAIN and every edit command. Each step brings the exact samples back. The history holds up to 192 MB by default, and the oldest steps are dropped first. Every change PROCESS makes is also handed to the Lab as its new sound, so UNDO in the Lab brings the earlier sound back too. When the Lab's sound changes outside PROCESS, PROCESS's own history is cleared.

Limits and notes

  • No single process makes more than 600 s of audio. The status line then notes "output capped at 600 s".
  • Spectral processes and the stretchers read the real audio around the region, so the region's edges sound natural.
  • Status notes you may see: "no wavecycles found (silence or noise?)", "RMS normalise limited by the peak (0 dBFS)", "stretch limited to 0.125x..8x (EXTREME STRETCH goes further)", "cross synth: no B sound (copy one, or leave audio after the region)", "out of memory: the result is too large".
  • The chain, the seed, the EDGE value, AUTO, the snap choice and the selected process are saved with your project. The audio itself is saved as the Lab's sound.
  • Leaving the page stops playback.

MIDI IN (note mapping)

With MIDI IN on, the page listens to the MIDI notes sent to the plugin:

  • Holding a note restarts the page and transposes it. Middle C (C4, note 60) plays the page as drawn; D4 plays it 2 semitones up.
  • The page starts again from the start, or from the loop start when LOOP is on.
  • Releasing the note stops the page.
  • Up to 16 held notes are remembered. The newest one sets the transposition; when you release it, the page uses the newest note still held.
  • An All Notes Off message stops the page.
  • Notes C-1 to G-1 (MIDI notes 0 to 7) switch to pages A to H instead of playing. MIDI IN is a per-page setting, so turn it on for every page you want to switch to and play.
  • While MIDI IN is on, only held notes play the page. HOST and PLAY do not.
  • The header shows MIDI and the held note, or MIDI IN: hold a note.

Speed lane

The strip under the canvas, labelled SPEED, bends the playhead's speed across the page.

  • Draw on it to make parts of the page run faster or slower. The range is x1/4 (bottom) to x4 (top), on a log scale; the middle line is x1.
  • Drawing on it turns SPEED on.
  • Values very close to x1 snap to exactly x1.
  • Right-click or double-click the lane to make it flat.
  • With SPEED off, the curve is shown dimmed and the lane reads "Draw here to bend the playhead's speed (SPEED turns it on/off)".
  • The speed lane also applies to rendered audio, MIDI export and SEND TO ARRANGE.

The line sound card

The card title tells you what you are editing:

  • LINE n when one line is selected.
  • n LINES (edits go to all of them) when several are selected.
  • NEW LINES START AS when nothing is selected. Edits then set the sound for the next lines you draw.

New lines start with:

  • the SFT waveform
  • a soft envelope (a short rise, a gentle fall, and a fade at the end)
  • LEVEL 0.8, UNISON 1, DETUNE 0.3, SPREAD 0.6
  • SUB 0, DRIVE 0, FILTER OFF, RES 0.2, RATE x1, REPEAT off

Double-click any knob to set it back to its default. While you drag a knob, its value pops up.

Waveform

  • WAVEFORM (ONE CYCLE) is the line's single cycle, 256 points. Click and drag to draw any shape.
  • The cycle is band-limited, and FM and DRIVE run oversampled, so it does not alias.
  • SIN, TRI, SAW, SQR, PLS, SFT start the cycle from a sine, triangle, saw, square, pulse (20% wide) or soft shape (a sine with a little 2nd and 3rd harmonic). You can then draw on it.
  • NOISE uses white noise instead of a cycle. If the line's filter is off, NOISE also sets BAND PASS, turns TRACK on and sets RES to 0.6, so the noise sweeps with the line like a riser.
  • SMOOTH rounds off the cycle (fewer high harmonics).
  • GRIT roughens the cycle with small random changes (more upper harmonics).

Envelope and pan

  • ENVELOPE is the loudness along the line, from its start to its end. Draw on it. The presets are:
    • FLAT: full level all the way.
    • SWELL: rises to full at 60% of the line, then falls to silence.
    • PLUCK: starts loud and decays.
    • FADE: falls from full to silence.
    • TREM: alternates between full and quarter level, 32 steps along the line.
  • PAN is the pan along the line; top = right, bottom = left. Draw on it. The presets are:
    • MID: centre.
    • L>R: left to right.
    • R>L: right to left.
    • WOB: 3 sweeps left and right.

Filter

  • Filter menu: FILTER OFF (default), LOW PASS, BAND PASS, HIGH PASS.
  • FILTER CUTOFF is the cutoff along the line. Draw on it. Without TRACK the range is 20 Hz (bottom) to 20 kHz (top). While the filter is off, this editor is greyed out and reads "pick a FILTER".
  • TRACK (default off) makes the cutoff lane relative to the line's pitch, so the filter follows glides. The middle of the lane is the line's own pitch; top and bottom are 4 octaves above and below.
  • RES (0 to 1, default 0.2) is the filter resonance.

FM, mute and timing

  • FM source menu: FM: none, or FM from line n for each other line on the page. The source line's sound bends this line's phase (line-to-line FM). If FM DEPTH is 0 when you pick a source, it is set to 0.3. The FM menu and FM DEPTH need a selected line.

  • MUTE: silences the selected lines; they still work as FM sources.
  • REPEAT (default off): the line loops at its own length for the rest of the page.
  • RATE: the line's own speed against the page. Options: x1/4, x1/3, x1/2, x2/3, x3/4, x4/5, x1 (default), x5/4, x4/3, x3/2, x2, x3, x4. A line at x3/4 against a line at x1 makes 3 against 4 (polyrhythm). With REPEAT on, the line loops at that rate.

  • A patch sets the waveform or noise, UNISON, DETUNE, SPREAD, SUB, DRIVE, the filter, RES, TRACK and the cutoff lane. Some patches also set the envelope.

  • With nothing selected, a patch sets what new lines start as.

DRAG MIDI

  • Drag it into your DAW. You get one note per line, and one note for each repeat of a REPEAT line.
  • Glides are pitch bend, the envelope is CC11 and pan is CC10. Velocity follows the line's loudest point.
  • Channels 1 to 16 are used, never channel 10.
  • A glide wider than the bend range is split into consecutive notes. When more lines overlap than there are channels, extra tracks are added.
  • The status line reports the number of notes, splits and tracks.
  • A click (no drag) opens "Save the page as MIDI" to save a .mid file (in your Music folder by default).

MPE (default off)

  • Exports as MPE instead: lower zone, master channel 1, one member channel (2 to 16) per voice.
  • The envelope becomes channel pressure and pan becomes CC74.

Bend menu: BEND 12 / BEND 24 (default) / BEND 48

  • The pitch-bend range of the MIDI export, in semitones. MPE doubles it: 24, 48 or 96.
  • The range is written into the file. Set your synth to match.

  • Click to render the page to a 24-bit WAV in the background. The render is one pass at your project's sample rate. It includes the speed lane and REVERSE, ignores the loop, and adds a 0.1 s tail.

  • When the render is done, the button reads DRAG AUDIO: drag it into your DAW.
  • If you change the page after rendering, the button goes back to RENDER AUDIO.

  • MIDI drags only the notes that start inside the loop, timed from the loop start.
  • A click on a grip only shows a hint.

  • The morph shape, MORPH AMOUNT and MORPH SMEAR only work while MORPH is on.
  • If a clip plays its whole sound, it changes length when PITCH (tape), STRETCH, KEEP LENGTH or WARP change the sound's length.

Morph

Turn on MORPH on a clip, then overlap it with the next clip on the same track. The overlap becomes a blend from this sound's spectrum into the next one's. Outside the overlap, both clips play unchanged. A MORPH → badge and a colour gradient mark the overlap.

Gain envelope

Press GAIN ENV or E, or choose Edit gain envelope in the clip menu. Then, on the selected clip:

  • click adds a point (snapped; Shift = off the grid)
  • drag moves a point
  • double-click deletes a point

Points run from -60 to +12 dB and sit on top of CLIP GAIN. The level is linear between points and holds after the last one. Fade handles are hidden in this mode. Esc, E or the button leaves it.

Loop region

  • Make a loop: right-drag on the ruler. It snaps to the grid and turns LOOP on.
  • Change it: drag the loop bar's ends to resize it (Shift = off the grid), or drag its middle to move it.
  • Drag it out: the ⇡ WAV and ⇡ MIDI grips on the loop bar drag the loop out as audio, or as the notes inside it. The first WAV drag starts a render ("Rendering the loop... drag its WAV grip again in a moment.").
  • When it plays: the loop works only when ARRANGE plays on its own (PLAY in SYNC: DAW with the DAW stopped). While your DAW plays, or in SYNC: OWN, ARRANGE follows that timeline and the DAW's own loop.

Markers

The thin strip at the top of the ruler holds the markers.

  • Add: double-click the strip, or right-click it and choose Add a marker here. New markers are named INTRO, BUILD, DROP, BREAK, OUTRO in the order you add them; after that they are all named OUTRO.
  • Use: click a marker to move the playhead to it. Drag it to move it. Double-click it to rename it (capitals, up to 24 characters).
  • Right-click a marker:
    • Rename...
    • Delete
    • Loop this section (to the next marker)
    • Render this section: loops the section and renders it.

Ruler and view

  • Playhead: click or drag the ruler to place it. Right-click = play from here (snapped). Home = back to the start.
  • Position: the top-left corner shows bar.beat.16th, min:sec and the time signature (4/4).
  • Wheel:
    • The wheel scrolls up and down when the tracks don't fit, and left and right otherwise.
    • Shift+wheel scrolls left and right.
    • Ctrl/Cmd+wheel zooms.
    • Alt+wheel sets the track height (36 to 160 px, 64 to start).
  • Zoom keys: + / - zoom, Z zooms to the selection, and FIT shows everything.
  • While playing, the view follows the playhead.

Getting sounds in

  • SEND TO ARRANGE on another page, IMPORT, FROM BEATS, or drop files on the timeline. Files can be WAV, AIF, AIFF, FLAC or OGG, but not MP3.
  • Where a sound lands:
    • A dropped file lands where you drop it, snapped. Drop below the last track for a new track.
    • Otherwise it goes on the selected clip's track, or the first empty track, or a new track.
    • It starts at the playhead (snapped), or after the clip that is already there.
  • Sounds longer than 20 minutes are cut to 20 minutes, with a message.
  • Re-rendering: clips from SPECTRAL LAB, ARCS and VOICE can be re-rendered from their page. Imported files can be re-read if the file is still there. PAINT PADS / LOOP sends ("Pad A", "Loop B"...) and bounces can't be re-rendered. If the page can't do it, the status line says why. The audio is saved with the project either way.

MIDI clips

Sounds that carry notes (for example from ARCS) show a MIDI tag. The MIDI chip writes one MIDI track per ARRANGE track, with its name, plus the tempo and 4/4. Track 1 uses channel 1 and so on, skipping channel 10. The notes follow each clip's position, trims, PITCH, STRETCH, WARP, REVERSE and LOOP. Muted clips and muted (or un-soloed) tracks are left out.

2.8 VOICE

VOICE takes a sound apart into its pitch and its formants (the resonances that make vowels and the "size" of a voice), lets you change them, and puts it back together. Load a voice, or any sound (a synth line, a chord or pad, a bass, drums), into slot A. Five modes at the top change it: PITCH, BORROW, CONTOUR, VOWEL and TUNE (keys 1 to 5).

The tab's tooltip: "Pitch and formants of a voice: move, borrow, draw, vowels, tune, double, harmony".

  • Top bar: the page title, the five mode buttons, UNDO, REDO and the i button.

3. Workflows

3.1 A pad that never stops

  1. PAINT PADS. Pick the key and scale first (or press DETECT KEY while your song plays).
  2. Turn on SNAP TO KEY. Click Ambient pad under STARTING POINTS.
  3. Raise BLOOM and SPACE, add SHIMMER. Keep HOLD on so it always sounds.
  4. Press FREEZE at the end of a section to hang the sound in the air. STOP fades it out; a DAW stop and play do the same.

3.2 Draw a bass and drums loop

  1. PAINT LOOP. Length box: 2 bars. Click a Bass in SYNTHS, choose DRAW, drag a line.
  2. Turn on QUANTIZE (grid 1/8) so notes snap.
  3. Click a kick in DRUMS, choose DROP, click on the beats. Each sound is its own layer; adjust its VOLUME and PAN.
  4. Press PLAY, or play your DAW. DRAG STEMS OUT gives one WAV per layer that has notes.

3.3 Turn a picture into a texture

  1. SPECTRAL LAB, IMAGE page. LOAD PICTURE.
  2. Set LOW Hz, HIGH Hz and CONTRAST. FIT TO 2 BARS to match your tempo.
  3. Press PLAY. Switch to the PAINT page and SMEAR or SHIFT parts of it.
  4. SEND TO ARRANGE.

3.4 Make a one-note sound playable

  1. SPECTRAL LAB: IMPORT SOUND. Check the root box ("plays at ...").
  2. Press KEYS at the top. Play your MIDI keyboard. The root key plays the sound as painted.
  3. Choose KEYS PITCH: SPECTRAL to keep the length, or TAPE for a sampler feel. Turn on ONE-SHOT for drums.

3.5 Variations on every hit (ghosts)

  1. SPECTRAL LAB with a short sound on the canvas. GHOST page.
  2. Set HITS to 8, raise EVOLVE, press GENERATE.
  3. Play KEYS: every key press plays the next ghost. Set the hit box to ALWAYS GHOST 3 to stay on one.
  4. SEND TO BEATS saves the whole set, one WAV per hit.

3.6 Reverse and stretch a sound (PROCESS)

  1. SPECTRAL LAB: make or import the sound. Open PROCESS.
  2. Drag across the tail of the waveform to select it (a plain click only places a cursor; PREVIEW and APPLY need a region). + ADD REVERSE, then + ADD STRETCH.
  3. PREVIEW, use PLAY SRC / PLAY FX to compare. BOUNCE NEW keeps the original in the SOURCE menu.

3.7 A synth line you draw (ARCS)

  1. ARCS. FACTORY for a starting page, or pick DRAW and sketch a line.
  2. Pick a PATCHES preset for the sound. Turn on QUANTISE and set the key.
  3. For a glide to a note use LINE. For a riser use CURVE.
  4. DRAG MIDI to your DAW and play it on your own synth, or RENDER AUDIO then DRAG AUDIO.

3.8 Make a synth speak (VOICE, BORROW)

  1. VOICE. Load a synth pad into A and a spoken line into B.
  2. Choose BORROW. Set the carrier to VOCODE for chords, or RESYNTH for a single line.
  3. Turn CONSONANTS on to hear s, t and k come through. PLAY RESULT. DRAG WAV OUT when done.

3.9 Build a song section (ARRANGE)

  1. Use SEND TO ARRANGE from three or four tabs.
  2. In ARRANGE, drag clips into place, add markers with a double-click on the top strip, and right-drag the ruler to loop a part.
  3. Use the clip PITCH and STRETCH to fit the key and tempo. Overlap two clips on one track and switch on MORPH for a spectral blend.
  4. Press RENDER, or drag the MIX chip into your DAW.

3.10 Tune a sung line to a key (VOICE, TUNE)

  1. VOICE. Load the line into A (or press TRY IT). Pick the Voice range (or Deep voice for low voices).
  2. Choose TUNE (key 3 is CONTOUR; TUNE is key 5). Set ROOT and SCALE to your song.
  3. RETUNE 40 ms is natural; 0 is instant and robotic. Raise FLATTEN to remove vibrato.
  4. If one phrase is wrong, select it in the pitch view and use the repair buttons (+12, -12, VOICED, UNVOICED).
  5. PLAY RESULT, then DRAG WAV OUT.

3.11 Thicken a voice (DOUBLE and HARMONY)

  1. VOICE with a voice in A. Set the DOUBLE box to x4 and raise WIDTH.
  2. Set H1 to 3rd up and H2 to 5th up; keep their levels around 0.7. Set ROOT and SCALE so the harmony stays in the key.
  3. PLAY RESULT, A/B (TAB) to compare with the original at matched loudness.

3.12 Band rhythms (SPECTRAL LAB, SPLIT)

  1. Put a pad or drone on the Lab canvas. Open the SPLIT page.
  2. Choose 4 bands. Use EDIT STEPS to switch steps off in the low band for a pulse and leave the top band running.
  3. Use EDIT CHANCE for random hits and EDIT PITCH to move steps by up to 12 semitones. Raise SWING. DICE rolls the chance steps again.
  4. Set HEARING to follow the page (or pin Split) and press PLAY.

3.13 Morph two sounds by playing harder (DNA)

  1. SPECTRAL LAB, DNA page. LOAD A and LOAD B (or CANVAS > A, CANVAS > B).
  2. Turn on PER NOTE and KEYS. Soft keys give A, hard keys give B.
  3. Or turn on PER BAR and draw the 8-bar lane for a morph that moves along the loop.
  4. MORPH > CANVAS puts the current morph on the canvas so you can paint on it.

3.14 Generate ideas with CHAOS

  1. SPECTRAL LAB, CHAOS page. Set the key and scale on the left.
  2. Set HARMONY to the middle (notes of the key), DENSITY to taste and CHAOS low for tidy, high for wild.
  3. GENERATE. Like part of it? Use LOCK TOOL to draw a box around it, then GENERATE again: locked boxes stay. NEW SEED rolls the dice.

3.15 Jam without a DAW (SYNC: OWN)

  1. Set SYNC: OWN, tap a tempo with TAP, press PLAY (or SPACE).
  2. PAINT LOOP, ARCS and ARRANGE now follow Spectrum's own clock. The Standalone app starts like this by itself.
  3. Switch to SYNC: DAW again when you want to follow your project.

3.16 One long note drives a whole section (PLAY: ON NOTES)

  1. Set PLAY: ON NOTES. Put a long note on the piano roll for the bars where Spectrum should sound.
  2. Mute or unmute that DAW channel to start and stop all the players in time with the song.

4. FL Studio tips

  1. Load it. Install, then in FL Studio open the plugin manager and run Find installed plugins (rescan). Add Ghosty Spectrum as a generator from the plugin database. On Mac, FL Studio loads the VST3 and AU versions.
  2. MIDI. Spectrum takes MIDI notes on its channel (all MIDI channels are accepted). The sound comes out of its stereo output. It sends no MIDI out.
  3. One long note for PLAY: ON NOTES. Open the channel's Piano roll, draw one long note over the bars where Spectrum should sound, and set PLAY: ON NOTES in the top bar. Muting the channel then stops the Lab loop, ARRANGE and ARCS; unmuting starts them again in time with the song.
  4. Tempo sync. Keep SYNC: DAW (default) to follow FL's tempo and play position. Use SYNC: OWN to try Spectrum while FL is stopped.
  5. Automation. Right-click a parameter in the plugin's wrapper (or use the wrapper's parameter list) and link it to a controller or create an automation clip. The full list is in Appendix A.
  6. Dragging out. Drag the pill or chip (such as DRAG PATTERN OUT (WAV), DRAG MIDI, MIX) onto the Playlist or a channel. Long renders start in the background; the pill says when it is ready. Drag-outs need the full version.
  7. Sidechain for DETECT KEY. Route another mixer track into Spectrum's input if you want DETECT KEY to listen to your song. Without a signal it listens to Spectrum itself.
  8. Saving. The whole state (all patterns, sounds, Lab canvas, ARCS pages, ARRANGE session, VOICE slots, PROCESS chain, look, SYNC and PLAY settings) is saved inside the FL project. A reopened project in demo mode starts at the default sound.
  9. Tail. Spectrum reports a 6-second tail to the host and no latency (ARCS lines come out about 0.75 ms late, which the plugin does not report).
  10. Standalone. Use the plugin's audio and MIDI settings to pick your sound card and keyboard.

5. The looks

Spectrum has three looks: BLACK, PURPLE and LIGHT. The fourth look, GHOST (liquid glass), is not in Spectrum; it belongs to Ghosty Pro.

  • Click the look button at the right of the top bar. Its tooltip reads: "The look of every page: BLACK -> PURPLE -> LIGHT (the button shows the next one; saved with the project)".
  • Each click goes to the next look (BLACK, then PURPLE, then LIGHT, then BLACK again). The button label always names the NEXT look. A new project starts in BLACK.
  • The look changes every page, including ARCS, ARRANGE, PROCESS and VOICE, whose own look buttons are hidden inside Spectrum.
  • The look is saved with the project, not in presets or canvas files.
  • The PAINT PADS canvas stays a dark night sky in all three looks.
  • A few text lines inside the HOW IT WORKS cards still say "DARK / LIGHT"; the button really has the three looks above.

6. Demo vs licensed

Spectrum runs unlicensed in demo mode. You can paint, play and edit everything. The limits are:

  • Drop-out: the sound goes silent for 2 seconds every 20 seconds (with a 30 ms fade at each edge). It is applied to the plugin's main output, so every page is affected (the PROCESS page's own audition is not).
  • Reopened projects start at the default sound. The saved sound comes back once you unlock.
  • No export. Audio and MIDI cannot leave the plugin. These 26 paths are refused:
    • PAINT: DRAG PATTERN OUT (WAV), DRAG STEMS OUT.
    • SPECTRAL LAB: DRAG WAV OUT, the waveform strip's WAV grip, SEND TO BEATS.
    • PROCESS: DRAG.
    • ARCS: DRAG MIDI, saving the MIDI by click, RENDER AUDIO, DRAG AUDIO, the loop WAV and loop MIDI grips, and the loop's background pre-render.
    • ARRANGE: the MIX, STEMS and MIDI chips, the loop WAV and MIDI grips, RENDER, SEND TO BEATS, and the automatic background MIX render.
    • VOICE: DRAG WAV OUT, the waveform strip's WAV grip, DRAG SET, RENDER SET, and RENDER SET to the shared folder.
  • When you try, the UNLOCK button blinks and a small hint appears near the top: "Export is part of the full version. Click UNLOCK and enter your license key (or get one at ...)", ending with the store address. Nothing is written and nothing is dragged.
  • Saves leave out audio the plugin made while locked: ARRANGE bounces and renders, the Lab's PROCESS result, and the canvas when it was rendered into morph slot A. Sounds you imported yourself (ARRANGE clips, morph slot B) are kept. Clips sent to ARRANGE from other pages are plugin-made, so they are left out of a locked save too. SEND TO ARRANGE itself is not blocked.

Still available in demo: all painting and playing, ARRANGE playback, PNG and SAVE PICTURE in the Lab, SAVE and LOAD of .ghostspec and .ghostarcs files, and saving the host project.

To unlock. Click the UNLOCK button (top row of PAINT PADS and PAINT LOOP; it is only shown in builds that need a key). In the "Unlock" box paste the license key from your purchase receipt email and click Unlock. Choose Buy if you do not have one yet, Cancel to leave. The first unlock needs the internet; after that it works offline. The key counts one use per computer, up to 5 computers; past that you get the message "This key is already used on N computers (limit 5). Email support to reset it." A bundle key unlocks every GhostBlock plugin on that computer with a single use. Spectrum re-checks quietly every 14 days and only locks again if the purchase was refunded or charged back, never because the internet is down. When unlocked the button reads LICENSED; hover it to see the key hint, or click it for a thank-you box.

The license file is kept in ~/Library/Application Support/GhostBlock (Mac) or %APPDATA%\GhostBlock (Windows).

8. Troubleshooting

1. No sound at all. Check the top bar. BYPASS silences everything. SYNC: OWN ignores your DAW, so press Spectrum's own PLAY. PLAY: ON NOTES only sounds while a MIDI key is held. On PAINT PADS, a STOPped pad needs START (or a touch on a note, a key, or your DAW's play). Check the instrument is routed to a mixer track.

2. Notes from my keyboard do nothing. Make sure the MIDI channel in your DAW goes to Spectrum. In the Lab press KEYS. On PAINT PADS turn HOLD off if you want the pad to follow your keys. In ARCS turn on MIDI IN. Opening the Lab stops the pad, so there is no drone under it.

3. The sound drops out for 2 seconds. That is demo mode (every 20 seconds). Enter your license key.

4. Drag-out does nothing and says "full version". Exports are part of the full version. Click UNLOCK and enter your key.

5. The plugin is not in FL Studio. Open the plugin manager and run a rescan (Find installed plugins). Make sure you installed the VST3 (on Mac: VST3 or AU). If you moved the plugin file, rescan again.

6. Mac: "cannot be opened because it is from an unidentified developer" (Gatekeeper). Right-click the installer or plugin file, choose Open, then confirm Open again. You only have to do this once.

7. Nothing plays when I press play in my DAW. Switch SYNC to DAW. Check PLAY is not on ON NOTES without a held note. In ARCS check MIDI IN is off (while it is on, HOST and PLAY do nothing).

8. A pause when I play a new key in the Lab. KEYS PITCH: SPECTRAL renders other keys on the fly. The first press of a new key plays TAPE while it renders. Choose TAPE for instant response.

9. PROCESS says there is nothing to process or PREVIEW is greyed out. Make or import a sound in SPECTRAL LAB first (or press TRY IT on the PROCESS card). Then drag across the waveform to select a region: a click only places a cursor.

10. A hint bubble keeps coming back. It shows once per tab per project. Press GOT IT, or click the page.

11. The tempo in a drag-out is wrong. In SYNC: DAW the drag-outs, stems and loops use your project's tempo. In SYNC: OWN or the Standalone app they use Spectrum's own tempo (the BPM box).

12. My dropped sample does not appear. Files dropped on the PAINT pages go to MY SAMPLES in PAINT LOOP. On SPECTRAL LAB, ARRANGE and VOICE the page takes the drop itself (Lab: the canvas or DNA slot; ARRANGE: a track; VOICE: slot A or B). Check the file type: the Lab and PAINT LOOP take WAV, AIFF, FLAC, OGG, MP3, M4A, CAF; ARRANGE refuses MP3.

13. VOICE gets the pitch wrong in a part. Select the part in the pitch view and use the repair buttons (+12, -12, VOICED, UNVOICED). Try the other pitch range, or switch FAST off for QUALITY tracking.

14. The reopened project sounds different or empty. In demo mode a reopened project starts at the default sound, and plugin-made audio is left out of saves. Unlock to get everything back.

9. FAQ

How do I hear only part of a sound? SPECTRAL LAB: right-drag in the waveform strip (or SELECT a box), then PLAY with LOOP SEL on, or HEAR. ARRANGE and ARCS: right-drag on the ruler. PROCESS and VOICE: drag on the waveform.

How do I undo? Ctrl/Cmd+Z or the UNDO button on SPECTRAL LAB, PROCESS, ARCS, ARRANGE and VOICE. PAINT PADS and PAINT LOOP have no undo: use patterns A-H (COPY first) to keep versions.

Does TRY IT delete my work? PAINT PADS and LOOP: the example goes into an empty pattern when the current one has notes. SPECTRAL LAB: the example is a NEW canvas (UNDO brings yours back). ARCS: one undo step. ARRANGE: two clips are added. VOICE: replaces slot A.

Where do my files go? See Appendix D: Documents/Ghosty/Spectrum for saved canvases, Lab bounces and images; Documents/Ghosty/Shared Sounds for SEND TO BEATS; temporary drag-out files in the system temp folder.

Can I use my own samples? Yes. Drop audio files on the PAINT pages: they appear in MY SAMPLES in PAINT LOOP. You can also IMPORT SOUND into the Lab, import files in ARRANGE, and load files into VOICE.

Does it send MIDI out? No. ARCS and ARRANGE can drag MIDI files out (full version), which you then play on another instrument.

Can I use it as an effect on my tracks? No. It is an instrument. Its sidechain input is only used by DETECT KEY.

What does the DAW tempo change? In SYNC: DAW, the PAINT LOOP pattern, the Lab's loop, ARRANGE and ARCS follow your project's tempo. Drag-outs and stems use it too.

How do I share sounds with Ghosty Beats? SEND TO BEATS (Lab, ARRANGE) writes into Documents/Ghosty/Shared Sounds. Ghosty Beats lists that folder, and ARRANGE's FROM BEATS reads it.

Which parameters can I automate? See Appendix A.

Is there a GHOST look in Spectrum? No. Spectrum has BLACK, PURPLE and LIGHT.

Does the output level have a knob? The plugin has a host-side Volume parameter (default 0.8, applied with a 1.25 gain) that you can reach from your DAW's parameter list. On screen use the pad LEVEL and the Lab LEVEL.

How many computers can one key unlock? Five. A bundle key unlocks every GhostBlock plugin on a computer with one use.

10. Glossary

  • Pad / pad dot: one ringing note on the PAINT PADS canvas.
  • Brush: the sound of new pad dots: BELL, PIANO, PAD, FEEDBACK.
  • Pattern A-H: one of eight saved pictures per page.
  • Layer: one sound in PAINT LOOP, with its own VOLUME, TONE, SPACE, PAN.
  • Spectrogram: a picture of sound: pitch up, time across, brightness = loudness.
  • Box: a rectangle you drag on the Lab canvas to select, shift, freeze, lock or cut.
  • Ghost: a variation of the Lab sound, played one per hit.
  • Split / band: the Lab sound cut into frequency bands, each with its own rhythm.
  • Chaos: the Lab generator that paints a new picture from a seed.
  • Seed: a number that makes random choices repeatable.
  • DNA / morph: blending sound A into sound B.
  • Process / chain: a destructive edit on the Lab sound / a list of them run in order.
  • Arc: a line in ARCS; each arc is one voice.
  • Page (ARCS): a set of arcs. Eight pages A-H per plugin.
  • MPE: MIDI with one channel per note, so each voice can bend on its own.
  • Clip / track: pieces of sound on the ARRANGE timeline / the rows they sit on.
  • Formants: the resonances that make a voice sound like itself and make vowels.
  • Slot A / B (VOICE): A is the sound you change; B is the voice you borrow from.
  • Vocoder / resynth: two ways BORROW rebuilds A: filter A's real audio, or rebuild it from its pitch.
  • Shared Sounds: the Documents/Ghosty/Shared Sounds folder that Spectrum and Ghosty Beats both use.
  • Stems: one audio file per layer or per track.
  • Sidechain input: the extra input DETECT KEY listens to.
  • Demo mode: unlicensed use, with the drop-outs and no export.
